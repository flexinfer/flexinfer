**The pair never preempts.**
- `maxNumSeqs 2` means only two sequences ever run.
- Two ~20K background sequences, or one background sequence plus a 7K interactive one, fit the 44,650-token pool.
  Engine-reported KV usage peaked at 95.9 %.
- The original 52.9K-pool design has the same property: 2 × 24.4K < 52.9K.
- So the pair cannot exercise the KV-victim choice, or #61976's self-preemption fix.

What it measured instead is overload:
- The offered prefill exceeds the card: decode falls to 14–18 tok/s, and interactive requests queue.
- Under priority, the waiting queue is ordered `(priority, arrival)`. Interactive requests jump ahead of waiting
  background ones: p50 is 6.5 × lower and p95 4.5 × lower.
- The cost is starving the background: a 318 s gap, against the 60 s bar the main arms use.

## What the 0.33 source says (re-read before the run, as the amendment required)

Read from `vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py` at v0.33.0:
- **Seat admission.** The waiting loop still breaks at `num_running >= max_num_active_reqs`. That is the new
  `max_num_active_seqs`, which defaults to `max_num_seqs`. **No running request is ever evicted to seat a
  higher-priority waiting one.**
- **KV victim.** Under priority it is still `max(running, key=(priority, arrival_time))`; under fcfs it is
  `running[-1]`.
- **#61976.** A lowest-priority running request that preempts itself now `continue`s instead of `break`ing, so
  higher-priority running requests later in the list can still be scheduled that step.

None of this can engage without KV exhaustion, and no arm preempted. So the 0.32 → 0.33 change cannot have moved this
verdict. A 0.32 run would have read the same.

## Why the seat reserve stops short of bar 1

With the reserve, an interactive turn always finds a seat. It still shares every engine step with the one background
sequence that holds the other seat.

- **Most turns** overlap only that sequence's decode, and land at 0.60–0.70 s against 0.52–0.57 s idle, which is
  1.1–1.2 ×.
- **The slow 7–10 %** land at 1.6–2.2 s. These are turns whose prefill was batched with a newly admitted background
  request's ~2K-token prompt. With `max_num_batched_tokens 4096` both prefills fit one step, so the interactive turn
  pays for the background prefill too.
- **The arithmetic agrees.** The background seat starts a new request every ~35 s, and each prefill takes ~1.5 s, so
  about 1 in 10 interactive turns should collide.

The 1.3 × bar is reachable only if background prefill never shares a step with an interactive prefill. Two ways to get
there, both new envelopes that need their own run:
- a smaller per-step prefill budget for the lane (e.g. `maxNumBatchedTokens` 1024–2048, at a prefill-speed cost);
- a proxy gate that holds a background admission while an interactive request is in prefill.

## Decision rule (from the plan) applied

"C fails bar 1 → the reserve does not protect interactive turns on this posture; do not ship MR 4, record why, and
re-plan." Accordingly:
- **MR 4 is not shipped.** The workhorse keeps `fcfs` and no `flexinfer.ai/qos`.
- The re-plan options above are the owner's call.
- **What the owner is weighing.** Against today's behaviour (arm A), the reserve turns a 13–23 s interactive wait into
  0.6 s typical and 1.7 s at p95. The cost is 30 % of background decode. Background work keeps moving: its largest
  gap was 22 s.
- **vLLM priority on top (D)** neither helps nor hurts at this load. Its only measured effect is ordering the waiting
  queue under overload (B-kv), which it does at the background's expense.
